Combat Overview & Initiative

Awareness

Awareness is what determines the phase-order of the combat. This is a standard combat round's phase-order:

  1. Effort Allocation Phase
  2. Turn Phase
  3. (repeat)

When both the enemies and the party are aware of combat starting, begin with the phase-order listed above. In this mutually aware case, turn order starts with whichever party has the creature with the highest Mental stat.

When combat begins with an unaware party (either the players or enemies), that unaware party does not partake in the first Effort Allocation Phase. In this exclusively aware case, turn order starts with the "aware" party.

Effort Allocation Phase

In the Effort Allocation Phase creatures in combat clear their action slots, proc "every round" effects, and perform effort allocation. Using this allocated effort, creatures "spend" effort of the main 3 stat types on actions.

To allocate effort, we have 3 effort-type buckets. At the start of combat, these buckets start with an amount of effort equal to the creature's related stat value. Then, allocate your pool of allotted effort points into the 3 buckets.

* Allocate as many effort points as you have in your effort stat value.

The effort points in these buckets are the resources creatures use to perform different actions. The only way a creature can re-allocate these points is to take the re-allocate action.

Turn Phase

Turns are taken in clockwise order assuming the players are sitting in clockwise-ascending Mental stat order. However, if the players do not wish to sit in this orientation the GM can choose Left-to-Right, ascending Mental stat order, classic roll-initiative order, etc.

The creatures in combat then take their turns clockwise around the table performing actions. Combat turn details are listed below.

Combat Turns and Actions

On your turn, limbs are action slots. Pay an action’s effort cost to perform it, spending a matching limb slot. You may also choose to leave some action slots for the rest of the combat round by simply not using all your actions. In this case, you may perform the "reaction" type actions for those limbs at any time during the round.

Some limbs may be of more than one type, or explicitly list a limb-specific action that it can perform, but generally you will be performing actions from this list of standard actions.

Disambiguations and Tips

  • Defense action: Spend defensive effort to reduce incoming damage by performing the defensive reaction when being hit in combat. To do this, an arm action slot must be available.

  • Healing: There are many ways to heal a creature, the limiting resource is your dwindling max vitality between combats. Cur Vitality can be healed up to Max Vitality by rest or items, but cannot exceed it. See "natural rest" downtime action.

  • When talking about plain "Vitality", assume it to refer to Current (Cur) Vitality.

  • Many creatures have exploitable weak points. Declare what you're aiming for -- targeting a specific spot, like a weapon's durability or an existing wound, can pay off with extra damage or a strategic advantage.

Status Effects

Status effects have a Count, which determines how many times the effect repeats. Certain Status Effects also have Potency, which determines the magnitude of its effects when applied. Potency and Count are indicated by the status's ratio PotencyCount.

* Stack is another word for an applied status effect.

For example, a 32 burn stack has Potency=3 and Count=2, which indicates the enemy will take 3 burn damage for the next 2 turns according to the burn status description.

Additionally, when reapplying an existing status, add to both the Potency and Count as if it were a fraction. E.g. 12 + 24 = 36

See list of status effects for more examples.

Scars

A creature gains a scar when it's reduced to 0 Vitality, or explicitly stated by relevant description. Gaining a scar reduces your Attuned Vitality by 1. See "medical rest" downtime action.

Scars can either be attached to a limb, or not explicitly be attached to anything. For the latter, you cannot remove the scar by losing a limb it's attached to. Some scars can be healed by certain downtime actions or items, so be on the lookout for ways to patch up.
